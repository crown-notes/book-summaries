# Building a Second Brain

In our modern world, the amount of information we receive at any given moment has dramatically increased, and we often spend our time consuming all this information without focusing on filtering what is actually needed and retaining it for later use.

Enter the second brain, a digital or analog system that helps you keep track of all the important information you need in your daily life. It allows you to store information based on context and different areas of your life and work, linking everything together by context.

> [!NOTE]
> A second brain can be created in a digital note-taking app like Evernote, Obsidian, or Notion, or simply in a journal that you can carry with you everywhere.

## The core system

All second brain systems have four common steps that allow you to save, refine, and reuse information from your second brain. This method is known as C.O.D.E., an acronym for Capture, Organize, Distill, and Express.

- Capture the important information and save it in your second brain.

- Open your second brain and organize all the captured information based on its context or relevance to your life.

- Extract the core concepts from the information needed for your work.

- Apply the new knowledge gained to solve your problem, complete your work, and share your learnings with the world.

## The P.A.R.A system

In a second brain, the most important step is to neatly organize your information so that you can access it whenever you need. There are many different systems available for this such as linking, tags, zettelkasten, and more. The author of the book, Tiago Forte, has created his own system called P.A.R.A. Which again is another acronym that stands for Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives.

- Projects are short-term goals or tasks that need to be completed within a certain time frame.

- Areas represent long-term goals that don't have a dedicated due date and can continue indefinitely if desired.

- Resources are links, books, or anything that helps with projects and different areas of life.

- Archives is where completed projects and items that are no longer relevant are stored. They can be accessed easily if needed.

## Feynman and Hemmingway's system

Richard Feynman, one of the greatest minds of all time, invented a system he called his twelve favorite problems. He would continuously think about at least twelve different problems. When he discovered something new, he would apply it to all twelve problems to make progress in at least one.

Ernest Hemingway developed a writing process called the Hemingway Bridge. This process involves not stopping work until you have defined the next part of the project for your future self. This ensures you stay on track and don't waste time figuring out what you did yesterday and where to continue today.

## Other Systems

Software engineers, product managers, architects, and almost all professionals use the divide and conquer strategy to solve their problems. Dividing a bigger problem into smaller ones is an effective approach to solving problems easily.

Culinary chefs use a system called mise en place to run their restaurants. They organize all required ingredients in the order of use, allowing them to easily access what is needed, effectively serving as an extension of their minds.

## Converge and Diverge

When you close all connections with the outside world and focus solely on your work, you are in convergence mode. In divergence mode, you broaden your horizons, gather as much information as possible about the topic you are researching, and collect a wealth of information.

> [!NOTE]
> Before starting a new project, enter divergence mode to gather as much information as possible. Then switch to convergence mode to use the information gathered for your project.
