# Dopamine Detox

Dopamine is a chemical in your brain that is released when you feel happy. It's essentially a neurotransmitter that makes us anticipate rewards. It allows us to feel better and gives a sense of accomplishment whenever we do something that makes us feel good.

In reality, dopamine is not the pleasure chemical everyone claims it to be. As soon as a reward is received and dopamine is released, everything becomes dull and empty again. It's similar to a drug; the more you get it, the more you desire it. No amount of stimulation will make you feel fulfilled with your life, and most people are overstimulated.

If you become addicted to dopamine, you'll continue engaging in stimulating tasks while delaying important ones, leading to a constant cycle of dopamine hits. Your focus is a valuable asset, which is why marketers spend billions to capture your attention. Mobile apps are designed to hook you, and social media companies like YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram know that the more time you spend on their platforms, the more money they make through advertising.

The suggestion algorithms have their pros and cons as well. It becomes easier for us to find the content we want to see, but it becomes hard to stop watching since it's what we are most attracted to.

You're not the one using the internet; the internet is using you. In the modern day, due to the overload of information around us, it is very easy for anyone to hijack your dopamine levels and keep you hooked on what they want.

Posted a picture on social media? You keep checking for the total number of likes and comments it gets. Watching a video on YouTube? It will keep auto-playing similar videos. Uploaded your online blog? You'll keep checking your website traffic and other stats. Whenever you start to lose focus and divert your attention to social media or other online services, they hijack your mind to lure you in.

Sweet foods and drinks release dopamine, providing a sensation of relaxation. In earlier times, finding easily consumable food was a significant reward, and eating it released enough dopamine for enjoyment. Today, food is so readily available that the reward aspect has diminished. However, food companies ensure we continue craving sugary foods and drinks to trigger dopamine release.

Dopamine addiction can significantly impair a person's cognitive abilities. Studies have shown that long-term thinking enables individuals to make sound decisions regarding their life, future, and financial well-being. However, the external forces present in the modern world often lead to short-term thinking and instant gratification.
